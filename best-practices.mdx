title: "Best practices"
description: "The habits that make Kinetic actually pay off — clean data, honest statuses, and fast follow-up."
---

Kinetic is only as good as the truth inside it. These practices separate shops that get value from shops that just have another login.

## Keep statuses honest

- Move repair orders the moment the real world changes. A car sitting in "In repair" that's actually waiting on parts corrupts every report and target.
- Never park a job in the wrong status to hide it. If it's stuck, that's a blocker to surface — put it on the record.
- End of day: sweep your board so tomorrow starts accurate.

## One record per real thing

- Always search before creating a person or vehicle. Duplicates split history and confuse every downstream feature.
- Fill in phone, email, and preferred contact method — the messaging features and the AI agent depend on them.
- When the system suggests a duplicate, take ten seconds to look. Merging later costs more than checking now.

## Communicate from inside the app

- Use Messages for every customer text/email so history attaches to the repair order. Personal phones create invisible conversations.
- Set the customer's preferred channel on their record and honor restrictions.
- Respond to inbound messages from the shared inbox daily; triage unmatched items instead of letting them pile up.

## Let the AI help — and check its work

- Record a verdict on every Rivet finding; the review only gets smarter when you tell it where it's right and wrong.
- Read AI drafts before sending. AI drafts, humans decide.
- Ask the agent specific, named questions; verify against the cited records.

## Manage commitments, not memory

- Put promises in tasks with due dates on the repair order — "call customer," "check parts ETA" — so anyone covering for you sees them.
- Watch Today every morning; it's the fastest way to spot what's slipping.
- Close the CSI loop same-day: a low score answered within hours often becomes a good review.

## For admins

- Keep membership lean: Member by default, Admin only for people who manage the shop, Owners to a minimum.
- Review members quarterly and remove people who leave — access ends when employment does.
- Treat the authenticator challenge as protection, not friction — it's what stands between a stolen password and your customer data.
