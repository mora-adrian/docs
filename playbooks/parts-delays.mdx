## When to use it

The Parts tab shows parts as **Outstanding** well after you expected them, or a vendor tells you a date has slipped. The car is in <Pill tone="waiting">Parts Hold</Pill>.

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Confirm the facts">
    Open the repair order's [Parts](/repair-orders/parts) tab. Check which parts are **Outstanding**, and the summary card (for example **4 of 6 parts received**). **Not reported** means CCC ONE has sent nothing, not that the part is late, so call the vendor to find out. Call the vendor if the date is unclear.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Look for another source">
    Ask whether another vendor, a different part grade, or an aftermarket or used part is available and approved by the insurer. If so, treat it as a [supplement](/playbooks/supplements).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tell the customer early">
    Text or call. Say what is late, the new expected date, and what you are doing. Offer a rental if one is available under their claim.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Update the date and the notes">
    Add a note with the vendor's name, who you spoke to, and the new date. If the target date should change, change it on the repair order and explain why in the same note.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use the waiting time">
    See whether other repair work can proceed. Move the car on to the next status when it is physically ready.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a follow-up task">
    Create a task for the day the part is due: *Confirm part arrived*. Assign it to yourself.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Done looks like

The customer knows, the new date is recorded, and a task will bring you back on the day the part is due.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting until the customer asks.
- Promising the vendor's best-case date as the delivery date.
- Not recording the rental request or approval.
