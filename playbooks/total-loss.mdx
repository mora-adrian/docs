A **total loss** means the car would cost more to repair than it is worth, so the insurer pays the car's value instead of repairing it. It is the hardest conversation a customer has with a shop, so handle it carefully.

## How Kinetic flags one

A car appears in the **Possible Total Loss** lane beside <Pill tone="waiting">Supplement Hold</Pill> when any of these is true:

- You set **Repairability** to **Total loss** in the repair order's facts.
- CCC ONE classified it as a total loss.
- Its CCC ONE stage mentions a total loss.

The lane is automatic. You cannot drop a card into it.

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Confirm with the insurer">
    Only the insurer decides. Do not tell the customer it is a total loss until the adjuster confirms in writing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set Repairability">
    Set **Repairability** to **Total loss** on the repair order so the board reflects it. It stays blank until someone rules on it, and Kinetic never assumes a car is repairable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tell the customer with care">
    Call personally. Explain what a total loss means, what happens to the car, and what they should expect from the insurer. Follow up in writing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prepare the release">
    On the [Signatures](/repair-orders/signatures) tab send the **Vehicle Release, Waiver, & Hold Harmless**. It is used for total losses.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Collect what is owed">
    Record storage, teardown, or other charges on the [ARW](/repair-orders/arw).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Release and close">
    Arrange pickup by the insurer's salvage vendor or the customer. Record who took the car, and when.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What changes in Kinetic

- Total-loss jobs are **not** reviewed by Rivet.
- They are **not** sent satisfaction surveys.
- Their target-date cycle is shorter (the shop's total-loss cycle days).

## Common mistakes

- Telling the customer before the insurer has decided.
- Skipping the release form.
- Forgetting to bill storage and teardown.
