A **repair order** is one repair job on one vehicle. Kinetic assigns it a permanent ID at creation and keeps it forever — the same record follows the vehicle from first contact through delivery, payment, and follow-up. Every message, photo, task, estimate, and event attaches to it.

## The lifecycle boards

Navigation is organized around four boards that mirror a vehicle's journey through the shop.

| Board | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Opportunity** | Leads and estimates — jobs quoted but not yet authorized or dropped off. |
| **Repair** | Vehicles actively in the shop: authorized, in repair, waiting on parts, or in QC (quality control). |
| **Close** | Ready for pickup, delivered, or awaiting payment and final paperwork. |
| **Enrichment** | Post-repair follow-up: surveys, reviews, and record completion. |

Each board offers three desktop views — **Table**, **Board** (status columns), and **Timeline** — and equivalent row and card views on mobile. Summary counts and metrics come from the server, so they always reflect real data, not a filtered subset.

## Find a repair order

- Filter by status; search by customer, vehicle, claim, or repair order number; sort by target date or age.
- Press `Cmd + K` or `Ctrl + K` and type a repair order number or customer name to jump straight to the record.

## Peek at a record: the drawer

Click a card once and the record drawer answers *"what state is this vehicle in?"* without leaving the board: status, target date, parts received percentage, outcome likelihood, expected insurance payout, assigned staff, and the five most recent activity items. The drawer is read-only. Its one action opens the full record.

## The full repair order screen

Everything about one job, organized in tabs:

| Tab | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | Status, target and out dates, repairability, parties, and key facts. |
| **Parts** | Ordered, received, and backordered parts, with readiness percentage. |
| **Insurance** | Insurer, claim number, payer context, and supplement status. |
| **Files** | Photos, documents, and estimates. Upload from the desk or the floor. |
| **Messages** | The full text and email thread with the customer. |
| **Calls** | Call history and recordings. |
| **Activity** | The complete audit trail: every change, who made it, and when. |
| **Projects & Tasks** | Work items attached to this job. |

The header always shows the repair order number, the status, and a *past target* indicator, so urgency is visible at a glance.

## Create a repair order

1. Select **New repair order**, or use the command palette.
2. Link or create the **customer** and the **vehicle**. Always search first to avoid duplicates.
3. Add the **claim and insurer** details if the job is a claim, plus the target date, starting status, communication preference, and lead source.
4. Save. Kinetic assigns the repair order number from your shop's sequence and shows it immediately.

Kinetic flags likely **duplicates** during creation and lets you confirm or continue. Check those suggestions carefully — merging records later is harder than preventing a duplicate now.

## Status changes and CCC ONE

Your admin configures the statuses for your shop. A typical flow is Opportunity → Estimate → Authorized → In repair → QC → Ready → Delivered. Change a status by dragging the card on the board or editing status in the record.

If your shop is connected to **CCC ONE**, status updates from CCC arrive automatically and are mapped to your shop's statuses. CCC is authoritative for every field it supplies, so a CCC event can update the record for you. The CCC status label is shown as the source of truth — Kinetic never invents a separate status.

<Warning>
Do not park a repair order in the wrong status to hide it from a board. Status is workflow truth: reports, target calculations, and automated follow-ups all depend on it being accurate.
</Warning>
