Kinetic keeps three core record types. All three are searchable from the navigation and the command palette.

| Record | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **People** | Customers, claimants, and contacts at other businesses: phone, email, preferred contact method, and every repair order and message they are connected to. |
| **Companies** | Insurers, tow companies, dealers, vendors, fleets, and referral sources. A company can have its own contacts — people linked to it. |
| **Vehicles** | Year, make, model, trim, VIN (vehicle identification number), color, plate, and the full repair history for that vehicle, including market value when available. |

<Info>
"Customer" is not a separate entity — a customer is a person with a repair order. The same person record can also be a vendor contact or a claimant on another job. One person, one record.
</Info>

## Why records matter

Everything links back to records. When you text a customer, the conversation lands on their person record and their repair order. When a vehicle returns a year later, its record shows the earlier repair. Clean records are what make the Agent's answers, reports, and automations accurate.

## Working with records

Follow these practices every time:

1. **Search before you create.** Look up the person by name or phone, or the vehicle by VIN. A duplicate splits message history and repair history.
2. **Check duplicate suggestions.** Kinetic surfaces likely duplicates with evidence. Review the evidence before you confirm a merge — merges are deliberate, never automatic.
3. **Read relationship counts.** Record lists show how many repair orders, vehicles, or messages each record has. Counts tell you at a glance whether a record is real or a stub.
4. **Refresh vehicle value when it matters.** Where configured, a vehicle record shows a market-value range that feeds the expected insurance payout on the repair order. Refresh it when the situation changes — for example, when a job might become a total loss.

## Contact details and consent

A person's record stores their preferred channel — text or email — and any communication restrictions. Honor what is recorded. If a customer asked not to be texted, that preference lives on the record and applies to every repair order they appear on.
