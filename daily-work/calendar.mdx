The **Calendar** screen schedules the shop's real-world commitments: drop-offs, pickups, estimate appointments, deliveries, and errands. Appointments link to the customer and repair order so the schedule always has context.

## What you can do

- **Create appointments** with type (drop-off, pickup, delivery, estimate, etc.), time, assignee, and location.
- **Address suggestions** autocomplete service addresses from the customer record.
- **Drive-time estimates** help plan pickups and deliveries realistically.
- **Route planning** orders a day's stops sensibly; reorder stops when plans change.
- **Calendar feeds** subscribe personal calendars to the shop schedule so techs see their day in the calendar app they already use.
- **Deduplication** flags appointments that look like double-bookings.

<Tip>
Schedule against the repair order, not from memory. An appointment created on the repair order inherits customer, vehicle, and address context — no re-typing, and it shows up in the job's timeline.
</Tip>

## Production planning

The **Production** view (configured in Settings → Production) shows capacity, technician assignments, parts readiness, holds, and aging per status — the inputs a production meeting needs. Scheduling and load-leveling recommendations explain their constraints, so you can see *why* a suggestion was made before accepting it.

Safety- and quality-critical steps are never auto-completed: QC and sign-off stay bound to human evidence and checklist state.
