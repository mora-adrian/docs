The **Calendar** screen schedules the shop's real-world commitments. Appointments link to a customer and repair order, so the schedule always carries context.

## Appointment types

New appointments are grouped into three services:

| Group | Types | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Concierge** | Concierge in (tow in), Concierge out (tow out), Rental in, Rental out | Vehicle transport and rental coordination. |
| **Sublets** | Sublet out, Sublet return | Sending a vehicle to a sublet vendor and getting it back. |
| **In-Person** | Experience in (customer drop-off), Experience out (customer pickup) | The customer physically coming to the shop. |

Other types — Mechanical out/return, Coatings out/return, Suggested estimate — remain on existing appointments but are not offered for new scheduling.

## Create an appointment

1. Choose the type, date, and time.
2. Link the repair order — search by number, customer, or vehicle. Linking inherits customer and vehicle context and puts the appointment on the job's timeline.
3. Add the place: the field label adapts to the job — *Pickup address* for tow/rental in, *Drop-off address* for tow/rental out, *Address* for in-person visits, *Vendor or address* for sublets. Address suggestions autofill from the customer record.
4. Assign a driver or coordinator where the type calls for one.

## The day panel and map

Selecting a day opens its panel: every appointment in order, with links to the records. The **map** renders the day's stops geographically, with **drive-time estimates** between them.

- **Route planning** orders the day's pickups and deliveries sensibly; drag to reorder when plans change.
- **Deduplication** flags appointments that are identical for planning purposes — same job, same type — so double-bookings surface before a truck rolls.

## Calendar feeds

Each person can subscribe a personal calendar to the shop's schedule feed, so today's stops appear in the calendar app they already use.

## Production planning

The **Production** view shows capacity, technician assignments, parts readiness, holds, and aging per status — the inputs a production meeting needs. Admins configure capacities and the production columns in **Settings → Production**.

Scheduling and load-leveling recommendations explain their constraints, so you can see *why* Kinetic suggests a plan before accepting it. Safety- and quality-critical steps are never auto-completed: QC sign-off stays bound to human evidence and checklist state.
