---
title: "Rivet estimate review"
description: "AI review of body-shop estimates — missed operations, evidence, and a feedback loop that learns your verdicts."
---

**Rivet** reviews estimates for likely missed or under-represented operations — scans, masking, remove-and-install, blends, and other non-included work shops routinely leave unbilled. It's a second set of eyes trained on a large corpus of reviewed estimates.

## The workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Import an estimate">
    Bring an estimate into Rivet (from the Rivet screen or an estimate import). Estimates can also arrive automatically from your estimating system.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run a review">
    Start a review. Analysis takes a few minutes — it runs in the background, so you can keep working; the screen updates when it finishes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Work through findings">
    Each finding describes an operation Rivet believes belongs on the estimate, with claim-ready wording (the CCC-style line title, justification, and supporting reasoning).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record your verdict">
    Accept findings that are real; reject ones that aren't. A finding already billed shows as **"Already on estimate."**
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Why verdicts matter

Your verdicts are the feedback loop. Accepted and rejected findings are read back into later reviews for your shop — verdict by verdict, Rivet's recommendations get closer to your judgment. That's also why a re-review opens a *new* run beside the old one instead of replacing it: the history of what you decided is data.

<Info>
Review the reasoning, not just the recommendation. Rivet proposes — the estimator decides. It will never push a finding as certain when the evidence is thin; reject confidently when the photos or vehicle context disagree.
</Info>

## Photo analysis

Where estimate photos are available, Rivet's vision pass uses them as evidence — damage visible in photos can support (or kill) a finding. Upload clear, well-lit photos of the damage for the best results.

## Getting the most out of Rivet

- **Review every estimate**, not just big ones — small missed ops add up fastest.
- **Always record a verdict.** Skipping verdicts means Rivet can't learn.
- **Be consistent.** Two estimators ruling opposite ways on the same op sends noisy feedback; align in a team meeting when verdicts diverge.
- **Use the claim-ready text.** Findings are drafted in the language supplements expect — adapt, don't rewrite from scratch.
